## §3. WHAT IS ASSEMBLED FROM WHAT — and what I rejected

**First, the honest finding the ask's plausible reading does NOT survive:** the ask's suggested reading — *"the constraint/repair registry and the listener registry are currently keyed by ADDRESS, and a graph would let structure be declared between nodes rather than at flat addresses"* — **is not what the trace supports, and I reject it as the subject.** Three measured reasons.

1. **The store has no nodes to key.** The addressee is an array of segments (`['a', 0, 'b']`) walked by `read`/`write`/`delete`; `provident-native/contract.md:1409` is the grammar's home, and the segment domain is *"strings or non-negative integers"* (`§8.1`'s `AddressMalformed` row, `:3963`). A graph node would be a thing the store does not have, minted by the package over the store's private state — and **`A127` holds that state PRIVATE** (`surface.md §2.1` (e) item 1), so the package cannot enumerate the store's structure to build nodes from it.
2. **The registries are not flat-keyed in the way the reading needs.** A constraint registration is keyed by the **`{address, id}` PAIR** with an injective pair-key spelling (`storeroom-constraints/contract.md §5.1` `P-IM-1`; `attempt-lifecycle.md §2.4` item 2). The pair already distinguishes two constraints at one address, so the flatness the graph would relieve is **not** a flatness the registry suffers.
3. **`A122`'s loop is already a cycle by design, and `A124` supplies its bound.** `A122`'s write flow is *"kernel checks → repair → package surface w/ commit → repeat"*, and `A124` gives the cycle its two needs — **each pass lands a commit** (the join) and **termination** on the check passing or a repair being impossible. **A graph method that added a second ordering over the same acts would be a second spelling of a sequence the engine already declares — the exact thing `A127`'s own words refuse:** *"Inconsistency - all store-modifying acts should operate by method, not function"* (read at `contract.md §2.4` item 9's `A127` clause, `:1056`).

**WHAT THE EVIDENCE DOES SUPPORT — THE SUBJECT THIS RECORD ACCEPTS, AND ITS ONE REAL GAP.** The constraint engine's own composition is **already a graph problem stated in op vocabulary, and it is `OWED` precisely because the store cannot see structure.** `AttemptRecord.ops` is *"the composition's CALL LIST"*; the walk runs *"OVER THE OPS THE COMMIT REGISTRY HOLDS SINCE THE CHECKPOINT AND STOPS AT THE OP THE `checkpoint` MEMBER NAMES"*; and `RC-18` records the live hole — **two attempts at NESTED addresses** where one address is a prefix of the other *"may both stand live and one composition may undo the other's ops"*, answered only by `A102`'s `D4` boundary (the checkpoint), with `KC-7` refused **per address** and never kernel-wide (`storeroom-constraints/contract.md:334`, `:649`–`:669`).

**So the subject, stated precisely:** *an ASSEMBLY-TIME STRUCTURE DESCRIPTION — declared between addresses, resolved by the package, and consulted at the kernel's composition boundary — that tells the rollback walk which addresses are ANCESTORS/PEERS of an attempt's own address so that `RC-18`'s cross-attempt collateral reach becomes a declared bound rather than a ruled one.* **That is assembly, it is address-keyed (not node-minted), it touches no private state, and it adds no member.** **What is assembled FROM:** the addresses the store's own grammar admits and the pairs already registered at them. **What the assembled artifact IS:** a resolved ancestor/peer relation over addresses, held caller-side, consumed by `§2.6`'s composition — **not** a node graph, **not** a render tree, **not** a thirteenth member.
